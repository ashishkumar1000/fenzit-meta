# Decision log — SPEC-company-hierarchy

Decisions taken during planning (2026-11-16), with the four research dig agents (backend+DB, frontend, internet feature research, meta-repo history).

- **D1 — Zero stored-procedure calls in this epic** (user, 2026-11-16). Not merely "no new RPCs": hierarchy code paths call no existing RPC either. Advisory locks inline (`pg_advisory_xact_lock`), audit rows as direct inserts, migration DDL-only. `write_audit_row` is bypassed; its column contract is reproduced once and copied. Extension of the standing AD-3 amendment direction ("no new RPCs at all", 2026-09-27).
- **D2 — Manager approvals are app-only in v1** (user accepted recommendation, 2026-11-16). fenzit-web gains only the manager column/edit (CAP-6); web approval surfaces for managers are a non-goal. Rationale: leave approval is app-only today; the web owner console is hard-gated to owner; opening it to managers is a larger role-plumbing change.
- **D3 — One epic, two approval stories** (user accepted recommendation, 2026-11-16). Leave routing (story 3) and job routing (story 4) are separate stories in epic 23; the shared rails the India market expects (greytHR/Keka precedent) arrive within one epic rather than two.
- **D4 — Manager is a tree-derived capability, not a role value** (accepted recommendation). The roles-and-access permission map is role-keyed and cannot express row-scoped power; the guard gains a scope dimension (role × subtree) instead of a new membership role. `permissions.ts` remains the single source.
- **D5 — Decision-26 exception recorded** (on the record per roles-and-access spec). Subtree leave visibility for managers is a deliberate second exception to "non-admins read only their own leave"; managers see leave reasons of their own people only.
- **D6 — Epic-22 SPEC revision recorded** (on the record per spec-employee-created-jobs). The Non-goals "multi-step or multi-approver approval chains" is superseded by this SPEC for the approval-routing dimension; the job decision path migrates from the `decide_pending_job` RPC to a NestJS transaction with an unchanged endpoint contract. The revision also covers epic-22 **CAP-7** (job visibility now includes the subtree manager) and the **approver identity in CAP-4/CAP-5** (the resolved approver decides, not "the owner"), plus the old contract's companion `job-approval-states.md` — its owner-labelled transitions and "Who sees it" column are superseded by the resolved-approver wording; this SPEC's job state diagram is authoritative, and the employee-facing label "Pending owner approval" becomes "Pending approval". Extended 2026-11-16 after the acceptance-auditor flagged the incomplete revision and the final review flagged the companion omission.
- **D7 — Submission and reminder notifications narrow from all-admins to the resolved approver set** (deliberate behaviour change, CAP-4). Admins keep list visibility and approval powers but stop receiving submission pushes AND the admin-named reminders (`leave.pending_reminder`, attendance reminders) unless they are in the resolved set. Dedupe keys keep the existing recipient-aware shape; recipients are computed before insert, so no duplicate rows arise. The narrowing rides the `fenzit-be` deploy — old clients render notifications from the database, so no client change is needed. Extended 2026-11-16 after the review flagged that D7 covered only submissions.
- **D8 — Unassigned manager falls back to the owner** (accepted recommendation). `manager_id = null` is allowed and means "reports to the company top" for routing; the migration leaves existing values null so behaviour is identical to today until owners assign managers. This also avoids inventing a "primary admin" choice for multi-admin companies.
- **D9 — Re-parenting on removal, not on demotion** (accepted recommendation; amended 2026-11-16 after the final review found it stale). Removing a member re-parents their subtree to the removed member's manager in the same transaction — falling back to all active admins when the removed member was unassigned, and refusing the removal if no active admin exists. A role change never re-parents and never removes rights the tree still grants (D13).
- **D10 — Never auto-approve** (industry research, on the record; amended 2026-11-16 after the final review). Requester-at-the-top self-decides (D11) — for a non-admin the walk's requester-is-approver case is unreachable, since the walk excludes R and the fallback set contains only admins. The classic silent failure across HRMS products is covered by D10+D11 together.

## Decisions from the BMAD code review (2026-11-16, four layers: blind-hunter, edge-case-hunter, verification-gap, acceptance-auditor)

- **D11 — The owner always self-decides their own requests** (user, 2026-11-16). The owner's leave/job submission stays pending in their own queue and the owner acts on it; logged as self-decided, never auto-approved. (The walk as first written dead-ended at the root.)
- **D12 — All active admins are the fallback set** (user, 2026-11-16). An unassigned member — and any walk that falls back with no resolvable single approver — routes to ALL active company admins: every admin may see and decide, notifications reach all admins (today's behaviour, preserved until real managers are assigned). No arbitrary single pick among admins.
- **D13 — Any active member may be a reporting manager; subtree rights are additive** (user, 2026-11-16). Employee, viewer, admin alike; subtree read/approve rights add to whatever the role already grants, so a plain employee becomes a working supervisor and a demoted member with reports remains a manager (capability is tree-derived, per D4). The assignment UI warns when assigning a viewer.
- **D14 — Manager apply-on-behalf routes up; never self-decide** (user, 2026-11-16; scoped 2026-11-16 after the final review). A manager filing a **leave** request on behalf of a team member creates a normal request routed to the nearest eligible manager above the subject — the actor never decides their own filing. On-behalf applies to leave in this epic; job on-behalf rides the future "managers creating jobs" story. The admin keeps today's immediate-approve behaviour for their own on-behalf filings (D11 covers the admin's own self-filed requests).
